# Mini-game redesign v1.0 — draft review

Content: `ptd-minigame-draft-2026-10-07`. Work is on `redesign/minigames-v1`; no live service or production database was changed.

## Design decisions and plan reconciliation

- The supplied plan's detailed rows contain **24**, not 21, practice stations (4/7/6/7). All named rows were retained. Four three-station boss rounds and eight final retrieval stations bring the learning log to 44 stations.
- The 24 practice stations receive 10 first-correct or 8 corrected reward, once, after review. Four case stamps award 15 each, six safety badges 5 each, and finishing the shift 25: maximum **355**, all-corrected **307**. Boss/final retrieval earns cosmetic stars and completion evidence, not repeated practice rewards. Outfit thresholds are **0/57/128/213/299** (rounded-up percentages). Old 150/250-point editions are unchanged.
- Tap/keyboard placement is available without dragging. Ring review uses accessible checkpoints and a supplied annotated normal reading example; abnormality selection follows the authored case report. It is **not** a validated diagnostic pixel-hotspot exercise. Answer-revealing supplied patient figures appear in feedback, with enlargement and source links.
- Form A/B final activities are authored alternatives covering the same objectives. Assignment is deterministic per saved attempt. Their educational equivalence needs faculty pilot review; no competence or exam-grade claim is made.
- Source regimens remain teaching comparisons. The app does not instruct a junior learner to prescribe adult doses to the 15-year-old, perform sensitive examination independently, force hip rotation, or run definitive haemorrhage procedures.
- Existing v1–v3 content files were not edited; saved attempts retain their own questions, translations and reward scheme. New attempts use v4.

## Required educator approval

1. Clinical wording, source classifications, simplified schematic anatomy and learner-level supervision.
2. Fluid discrepancy: S23 fixed 2 L versus current [NICE NG39](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng39/chapter/Recommendations). The scored action uses senior/local haemorrhage protocol rather than requiring the old load.
3. Antibiotics: H19 versus S29 doses; Grade III cefazolin ambiguity; age/weight/allergy/renal and local policy. H19 ≤3 h versus [BOAST open fracture](https://www.boa.ac.uk/resource/boast-4-pdf.html) ideally ≤1 h.
4. Binder: H18 normal-AP removal versus [BOAST pelvic fracture](https://www.boa.ac.uk/resource/boast-3-pdf.html) masking of instability and safe removal protocol. Conceptual snugness is not a measured pressure.
5. S16 25°/60° versus H13–14 30–45°; mortality/population ranges and source-specific flowchart thresholds. Do not turn them into an individual risk calculator or autonomous algorithm.
6. Image copyright, de-identification, accessibility and appropriate coverings. Newly extracted S16/38/41/44/50 figures were inspected locally; this is not institutional permission to publish.
7. Screen-reader/touch pilot; duration, cognitive load, A/B comparability and whether additional validated radiographic zones are useful. Image-zone validation is deliberately not fabricated.

These are formative learning activities and sources for in-class discussion, not a validated examination or proof of independent trauma competence.
