# Teacher rubric and in-class discussion guide

Use actual first answers, corrections and handover notes as starting evidence. This manual observation is formative and does not change reward or certify competence.

## Rubric anchors

| Dimension | Look for during discussion |
|---|---|
| Clinical interpretation | Connects supplied observations/report to concerns; separates physiological and mechanical judgments; does not invent findings. |
| Priorities and supervised action | Identifies urgent priorities, parallel assessment/support and appropriate senior/team request; stays within learner responsibilities. |
| Communication of uncertainty/request | Distinguishes observed findings from suspicion, states uncertainty and makes a clear request. |

Choose one descriptive state independently for each dimension:

- **Not observed:** no opportunity/evidence yet. Not equivalent to incorrect.
- **Needs further discussion:** explanation remains incomplete or concerning after discussion.
- **Explains with prompting:** connects the evidence with a teacher cue or question.
- **Explains without prompting:** gives an appropriate explanation during this discussion without a cue. Not a certification of clinical competence.

Save case- or safety-concept scope, observation, feedback and next step. The app records reviewer, server time, attempt and exact content version. Records are staff-only and append-only. Saving connected reviews requires confirmed authenticated service access; the local demo stores only fictional notes on the device.

## Before class

1. Check roster and received submission status. Missing responses may mean no start, an offline learner or a submission problem; do not count them as errors.
2. Review first-response misconceptions with counts/observed denominators and unresolved corrections. Keep reward separate from learning evidence.
3. Read the three handovers, prioritizing notes still awaiting discussion. Select advice that fits the learner rather than applying a canned diagnosis.
4. Download separate cohort, evidence and staff-review CSVs under institutional privacy rules. Print the anonymous class briefing; it excludes names, learner notes and private observations.

## In-class supported retrieval using the same cases

Ask learners to first explain from memory, then reopen the supplied chart and compare. These are discussion prompts, not new numbered pre-class questions.

- Patient 1: Which observations made the arrival urgent? What did the transient response change? Compare an imaging-first plan with parallel resuscitation and senior haemorrhage-control planning in the supplied remote-CT setting. Ask for a one-sentence urgent handover and explain uncertainty about the source.
- Patient 2: Reconstruct the arrival binder context and documented ring findings. Compare physiological stability with mechanical stability. What remains uncertain, and what supervised imaging/reassessment and removal-plan request follows? Discuss the H18/BOAST discrepancy with the approved local policy.
- Patient 3: Recall the perineal wound and later meatal bleeding. What does each finding suggest without proving? Compare isolated urinary planning with joint trauma/wound/urinary review. Ask learners to revise an overconfident referral into evidence, suspicion and an explicit senior request.

## Advice workflow

Use the node's authored misconception, discussion prompt and suggested feedback. Listen before choosing rubric states. Record a specific observation (what the learner actually said), one actionable feedback message and a next step (for example, compare two stability claims using the supplied report). Do not add patient facts or prescribe unsupplied definitive treatment.

For corrected answers, ask the learner to explain why the original alternative was tempting and which evidence changed their interpretation. For low or omitted confidence, invite uncertainty without penalizing it. For missing evidence, address participation/access rather than presuming a misconception.

The printed anonymous briefing works once generated, without a connection. Connected review saving and real cohort reports still require institutional configuration and verified assigned-cohort access.
